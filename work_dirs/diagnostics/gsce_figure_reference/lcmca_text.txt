LCMCA-Net: A Large-Kernel Contour Attention
and Multiscale Corner Alignment Network for
Remote Sensing Object Detection
Wentao Pan
and Xiaodan Lv
Abstract—Remote sensing object detection plays a crucial role
in national defense and security applications. Although anchor-
free methods such as CenterNet++ achieve competitive recall
and low false-positive rates, they remain limited by restricted
global receptive ﬁelds and insuﬃcient exploitation of geometric
contour information. In addition, center keypoints often yield
weak convolutional responses for objects with large homogeneous
regions or hollow structures and for small objects, increasing
the likelihood of missed detections and false alarms. To address
these limitations, we propose LCMCA-Net, which integrates a
large-kernel contour attention (LC) module and a multiscale
corner alignment (MCA) module. The LC module uses large-
kernel separable convolutions with stage-speciﬁc kernel sizes to
enlarge the receptive ﬁeld and capture ﬁne-grained contour cues.
The MCA module further uses parallel dilated convolutions
to predict spatial oﬀsets and modulation masks from center
features, after which deformable sampling dynamically aligns
the top-left and bottom-right features. The resulting geometry-
related corner information is incorporated into the center branch.
This integration compensates for weak center responses and
improves consistency between the center and corner represen-
tations. Extensive experiments on the two public datasets, DIOR
and NWPU VHR-10, show that LCMCA-Net attains state-of-
the-art performance among the methods evaluated on these
datasets. The source code is available at https://github.com/
aicat777/LCMCA-Net
Index Terms—Anchor-free detection, attention mechanism,
feature alignment, large-kernel convolution, object detection,
remote sensing.
I. INTRODUCTION
R
ECENT advances in deep learning have substantially
expanded the capabilities of remote sensing, support-
ing applications ranging from image classiﬁcation and scene
understanding to object detection. In parallel with conventional
artiﬁcial neural networks, recent research on spiking neural
networks has examined remote sensing scene classiﬁcation,
onboard object recognition, and event-based optical ﬂow esti-
mation. These studies point to the potential of spike-driven
computation for low-power remote sensing analysis [1], [2],
[3], [4].

Among these applications, remote sensing object detection
remains particularly challenging because scenes frequently
contain dense clusters of small objects and objects spanning a
wide range of scales [5]. Recent studies have approached these
challenges from several directions. For example, horizontal-to-
rotated box bridging has been investigated to improve object
localization, whereas frequency-aware coarse-to-ﬁne supervi-
sion has been introduced to strengthen feature representation
in complex remote sensing scenes [6], [7]. Bottom-up anchor-
free detectors have likewise attracted increasing attention
[8]. By eliminating predeﬁned anchors, these methods oﬀer
improved adaptability to objects with extreme aspect ratios.
For example, CornerNet achieves high recall by predicting
corner keypoint heatmaps, but it often suﬀers from a high
false-positive rate [9]. CenterNet++ alleviates this issue by
introducing an auxiliary center keypoint to verify corner pairs,
thereby markedly reducing false alarms [10].
Despite these advances, applying CenterNet++ to remote
sensing object detection still presents two critical limitations.
First, the backbone network has a limited global receptive
ﬁeld, which restricts the ability to model broad spatial con-
text and fully exploit the distinctive geometric contour cues
in remote sensing images. Second, center keypoints often
exhibit weak local convolutional responses for objects with
large homogeneous regions or hollow structures and for small
objects. As a result, valid candidate boxes may receive low
conﬁdence scores or be incorrectly classiﬁed as background.
To address these limitations, we propose LCMCA-Net, a
novel architecture built upon CenterNet++. The main contri-
butions of this work are summarized as follows.
1) We propose the LC module to enhance the fea-
ture extraction capability of the ConvNeXt backbone.
By integrating multistage large-kernel separable con-
volutions, the LC module eﬃciently enlarges the
receptive ﬁeld and strengthens contour-aware feature
representation.
2) The multiscale corner alignment (MCA) module is
designed to predict spatial oﬀsets and modulation
masks from center features through parallel dilated
convolutions and to dynamically align the top-left and
bottom-right features using deformable sampling. By
incorporating geometry-related corner information into
the center branch, the MCA module compensates for
weak center responses, improves consistency between

II. METHODOLOGY
A. Overall Architecture
CenterNet++ introduces an auxiliary center keypoint to
reduce false-positive detections in CornerNet. However, it still
struggles with large-scale variations and complex background
interference in remote sensing object detection. To address
these challenges, we propose LCMCA-Net. As shown in
Fig. 1, the overall architecture is built upon a feature pyramid
framework and integrates the proposed large-kernel contour
attention (LC) module and MCA module to enhance feature
representation and keypoint alignment.
B. Large-Kernel Contour Attention (LC) Module

patterns provide important visual cues for object detection.

complex spatial contexts. To address both requirements, the
LC module is introduced. As shown in Fig. 2, the LC module
embeds a multiscale large separable kernel attention (LSKA)
[11] mechanism within the ConvNeXt block [12].

C. MCA Module
At each FPN level of CenterNet++, the detection head
produces top-left, bottom-right, and center heatmap features
at the corresponding scale. The three conﬁdence maps and six
oﬀset maps generated by the top-left, bottom-right, and
enhanced center branches are subsequently concatenated and
injected into the classiﬁcation features and point-set reﬁne-
ment features. Finally, the deformable convolutions use oﬀsets
predicted from the initial point set to produce the ﬁnal classi-
ﬁcation and reﬁned regression outputs.
However, center points often produce weak responses for
objects with large homogeneous regions or hollow structures
and for small objects, increasing the likelihood of missed
detections and false alarms. The MCA module is, therefore,
applied to the center features at each FPN level. It uses the
corresponding top-left and bottom-right features to align and
enhance the center features. The module architecture is shown
in Fig. 3.
For the top-left branch, the MCA module processes the
center feature Fct using a multiscale spatial perception
block to estimate coordinate oﬀsets ∆Ptl and corner weight
masks Mtl

of center representations and reducing missed detections and
false alarms.
III. EXPERIMENTS
A. Datasets
Extensive experiments are conducted on two public remote
sensing object detection datasets, DIOR and NWPU VHR-10,
to evaluate the proposed modules.
The DIOR dataset contains 23 463 images and approxi-
mately 192 000 instances across 20 categories [13] (C1–C20
represent airplane, airport, baseball ﬁeld, basketball court,
bridge, chimney, dam, expressway service area, expressway
toll station, golf course, ground track ﬁeld, harbor, over-
pass, ship, stadium, storage tank, tennis court, train station,
vehicle, and windmill, respectively). The oﬃcial DIOR split
is followed, with the trainval set used for training and the
test set reserved for evaluation. To evaluate generalization
across diﬀerent data distributions, the NWPU VHR-10 dataset
[14] is also used. It contains 800 very-high-resolution images
from ten classes: N1–N10 denote airplane, ship, storage tank,
baseball diamond, tennis court, basketball court, ground track
ﬁeld, harbor, bridge, and vehicle, respectively. The dataset is
randomly divided into training and validation sets at a ratio
of 4:1.
B. Experimental Settings and Evaluation Metrics
1) Experimental Settings: All experiments are conducted in
PyTorch 2.0 on eight NVIDIA RTX A6000 GPUs with a batch
size of eight and an input size of 800 × 800. The models are
trained for 72 epochs using AdamW with an initial learning
rate of 2 × 10−4 and a weight decay of 0.05. A linear warm-
up is applied for 500 iterations with a warm-up ratio of 0.001.
The learning rate is then decayed at epochs 63 and 69. Random
ﬂipping and resizing are applied for data augmentation.

2) Evaluation Metrics: Following standard object detec-
tion protocols, we report the mean average precision at
diﬀerent IoU thresholds, including mAP50, mAP75, and the
more stringent mAP50:95. Scale-speciﬁc average precision met-
rics, including mAPS, mAPM, and mAPL, are also reported.
Together with the total number of parameters (Params),
ﬂoating-point operations (FLOPs), and inference speed (FPS),
these metrics characterize localization accuracy and model
complexity.
C. Experimental Results
1) Ablation Study:
Both parameter-level and module-
level ablation studies are conducted on the DIOR dataset
using a randomly initialized ConvNeXt backbone to assess
the individual and combined contributions of the LC and
MCA modules. The parameter-ablation results are reported in
Tables I and II of the Supplementary Material. Both sets of
results support the selected module conﬁgurations.
As shown in Table I, adding the LC module to the
CenterNet++ baseline improves mAP50:95 by 1.8 percentage
points, whereas the MCA module alone yields a gain of
0.4 percentage points. When the two modules are combined,
mAP50:95, mAP50, and mAP75 increase by 2.2, 1.9, and
2.0 percentage points, respectively. The combined conﬁgura-
tion also increases mAPS and mAPL by 3.7 and 1.4 percentage
points, respectively. The LC and MCA modules individually
increase the parameter count by 7% and 1%, respectively.
Despite this overhead, their combination achieves the highest
detection performance among the tested conﬁgurations at an
acceptable level of complexity. This combined architecture is
referred to as LCMCA-Net. Relative to the baseline, LCMCA-
Net improves mAP50:95 by 2.2 percentage points. This gain is
accompanied by a 6.2% increase in FLOPs and a reduction in
inference speed from 17.1 to 13.5 FPS.
2) Comparison With State-of-the-Art Methods: LCMCA-
Net is compared with a set of representative and recent
detectors on the DIOR and NWPU VHR-10 datasets, includ-
ing Faster R-CNN [15], CornerNet [9], RepDarkNet [16],
YOLOv11s [17], DINO [18], Co-DETR [19], PDQR-DINO
[20], DSDEF [21], and SED-DETR [22].
On the DIOR dataset, LCMCA-Net achieves an overall
mAP50 of 85.0%, as shown in Table II, exceeding DINO by
4.8 percentage points. It also attains the highest classwise AP50
for challenging categories such as airport and bridge.
On the NWPU VHR-10 dataset, LCMCA-Net achieves an
overall mAP50 of 95.9%, as shown in Table III, exceeding
DINO by 3.2 percentage points. It also attains the highest
classwise AP50 for the storage tank and tennis court categories.
3) Cross-Dataset Generalization Experiment:
A cross-
dataset transfer experiment is conducted to evaluate LCMCA-
Net under domain shifts and on unseen data distributions. In
particular, LCMCA-Net is trained on DIOR and evaluated
directly on the NWPU VHR-10 validation set without ﬁne-
tuning. The results reported in Table III of Supplementary
Material provide evidence that LCMCA-Net remains robust
under the evaluated domain shift and unseen data distribution.
4) Visualization: Fig. 4 presents a qualitative comparison
between CenterNet++ and LCMCA-Net on the DIOR dataset.

IV. CONCLUSION
This letter presents LCMCA-Net, a novel anchor-free detec-
tor designed for complex remote sensing imagery. To address
the limited receptive ﬁeld and severe false-positive responses
observed in baseline models, we propose two key compo-
nents. The large-kernel contour attention (LC) module expands
the global receptive ﬁeld to capture discriminative geometric
contour cues, while the MCA module dynamically aligns
corner features with center features to suppress false alarms.
Comprehensive experiments on DIOR and NWPU VHR-10
show that LCMCA-Net achieves high detection accuracy and
state-of-the-art results among the evaluated methods. The
cross-dataset evaluation further indicates robustness to the
tested domain shift. The cross-dataset results also suggest that
the model generalizes to the evaluated unseen data distribution.
Nevertheless, performance gaps remain between LCMCA-Net
and state-of-the-art models for complex categories such as
harbor and for densely distributed small objects in categories
such as vehicle. Future work will therefore focus on further
improving LCMCA-Net. To improve real-time performance,
future work will investigate removing cascaded corner pool-
ing and developing lightweight architectural variants. These
studies will assess the feasibility of deploying LCMCA-Net
in real-time and edge-computing scenarios.
